# What PMC9282141 supports about common food allergens in Asia

Date reviewed: 2026-09-08

## Bottom line

Yes, this paper can support a **provisional, age-separated Asian allergen list**, but it cannot support a definitive Asia-wide ranking and it provides no Cambodia-specific evidence.

The defensible union of allergens named as common in its Asia prevalence paragraph is:

- cow's milk
- peanut
- hen's egg
- shellfish/crustaceans
- finned fish
- wheat
- buckwheat
- tree nuts

Edible swiftlet bird's nest can be recorded as a **locally important or unusual trigger reported in Singapore and Malaysia**, not as a common allergen across Asia. The cited source also describes other locally important foods, but the reviewed paper itself names only buckwheat and bird's nest as distinctive Asian examples.

This evidence is best used to choose groups for further investigation. It is not sufficient by itself to decide a Cambodia-focused production classification.

## What the linked paper actually is

[Rotella and Oriel, *Accidental Reactions to Foods: Frequency, Causes, and Severity* (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9282141/) is a narrative review. It did not recruit an Asian cohort, collect participant data, or publish a machine-readable dataset. The relevant material is one paragraph under **Global Epidemiology > Asia**. There is no Asia table in this paper.

The paragraph combines two earlier reviews:

1. Its prevalence percentages come from reference 6, [Baseggio Conrado, Patel, and Turner, *Global patterns in anaphylaxis due to specific foods: a systematic review* (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8674817/), specifically its supplementary **Methods > Estimated prevalence of allergy to specific foods** and **Table E1**.
2. Its statements about buckwheat and swiftlet bird's nest come from reference 13, [Loh and Tang, *The Epidemiology of Food Allergy in the Global Context* (2018)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6163515/), especially **Section 6: Global Variation in Common Triggers of Food Allergy and Food-Induced Anaphylaxis**.

The numbers below therefore are not the linked paper's own measurements. They are its summary of pooled estimates from another systematic review.

## The exact ranked lists reported in the Asia paragraph

### Adults

The linked paper reports the following order for adults:

| Rank | Allergen | Pooled prevalence quoted by PMC9282141 | 95% CI in source Table E1 |
| ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | Cow's milk | 0.48% | 0.38–0.61% |
| 2 | Peanut | 0.46% | 0.36–0.58% |
| 3 | Hen's egg | 0.30% | 0.22–0.40% |
| 4 | Shrimp/crustaceans, called “shellfish” in PMC9282141 | 0.27% | 0–7.5% |
| 5 | Finned fish | 0.22% | 0.01–1.4% |
| 6 | Wheat | 0.14% | 0.04–0.53% |

Scope: the source systematic review says the adult estimates were pooled from **Taiwan and India only**. They do not represent all Asian adults. The very wide confidence intervals for crustaceans and fish also show substantial uncertainty.

Scope: the source review pooled studies from **China, India, Russia, Thailand, Japan, and Korea**. It did not include Cambodia.

Source Table E1 additionally reports wheat (0.06%), sesame (0.07%), soybean (0.07%), and kiwi (0.02%) for children. PMC9282141 does not include these in its ranked “most prevalent” sentence. Missing adult values for tree nuts, sesame, soybean, and several other foods mean “not estimated,” not zero prevalence.

## Why this is not an Asia-wide ranking

The cited systematic review explicitly says that Asian prevalence data are limited. Its pooled values combine different countries, ages, questionnaires, diagnostic definitions, and study periods. The underlying evidence includes:

| Source population | What the source actually measured | Important country/age signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taiwan, 30,018 people across children and adults | Nationwide questionnaire; physician screening of reported symptoms and medical records, not oral food challenges | Seafood—shrimp, crab, fish, and mollusc—was the most commonly reported category in the total population. Milk, shellfish, peanut, and egg were common in children. [Wu et al. abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22530688/) |
| Karnataka, India, adults aged 20–54 | 11,791 screened; 588 in a case-control stage; “probable food allergy” required symptoms plus specific IgE | Probable food allergy was 1.2%, mainly cow's milk (0.5%) and apple (0.5%). [Mahesh et al. abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27297800/) |
| China, India, and Russia, 35,549 children aged 6–11 | Standardized EuroPrevall screening and sensitization tests; published prevalence endpoint was “probable food allergy,” defined by symptoms within two hours plus sensitization | Overall probable allergy varied sharply by site: Hong Kong 1.50%, Tomsk 0.87%, Shaoguan 0.69%, Guangzhou 0.21%, and India 0.14%. [Li et al. study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213219819310323) |
| Thailand, 656 children aged 6 months–6 years | Parent questionnaire; 21 children underwent food challenge and 3 were positive | Cow's milk and egg were most reported in younger children; seafood, especially shrimp, in older children. Estimated challenge-confirmed prevalence was 0.45%. [Santadusit et al. record](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/entities/publication/08967844-3c77-4dfc-b537-5c2a34b05deb/full) |
| Northern Thailand, preschool children aged 3–7 | Parent questionnaire followed by skin testing, specific IgE, and challenge for a subset; 18 challenged and 5 positive | Reported leading foods were shrimp, milk, fish, egg, and ant egg; shrimp was the most common confirmed trigger. [Lao-araya and Trakultivakorn abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22168484/) |
| Japan, 92,945 children aged 1–3 | Caregiver-reported immediate allergy and caregiver-reported physician diagnosis; no challenge-positive endpoint | Egg was highest at each age, followed by milk and wheat; egg and milk decreased with age. [JECS study, Results and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7652713/) |
| Korea, 29,842 schoolchildren aged 6–16 | Nationwide questionnaire using a symptom/timing algorithm; no challenge-positive endpoint | Individual foods differed by age: egg at 6–7, peanut at 9–10, milk at 12–13, and buckwheat at 15–16. As groups, fruit, crustaceans, tree nuts, and fish were most frequent. [Kim et al., Results and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5500695/) |

These differences matter. An oral-food-challenge result is not equivalent to sensitization plus symptoms, an algorithmic questionnaire, or caregiver-reported physician diagnosis. Pooling them creates a useful regional signal, but not a precise, population-weighted ranking for Asia.

The source systematic review also analyzed **anaphylaxis presentations**. That is a different endpoint from prevalence: it reflects exposure patterns and severity as well as how common an allergy is. In **Results > Common food triggers for anaphylaxis compared to prevalence**, it reports fish and crustaceans as common adult anaphylaxis triggers in Asia. In the discussion it adds that wheat accounts for a disproportionate share of anaphylaxis presentations in China and that cow's milk causes a greater-than-expected share among Asian children. These observations support prioritizing those groups, but their percentages must not be mixed with population prevalence.

## Distinctive allergens: what can and cannot be claimed

PMC9282141 says:

- **Buckwheat** is a distinctive trigger, especially in South Korea and Japan.
- **Swiftlet bird's nest** is a distinctive trigger reported in Singapore and Malaysia.

Its cited review adds location-specific context: shrimp leads in a northern Thai child study; chickpea is important in India; fish, shellfish, buckwheat, bird's nest, and royal jelly have caused severe reactions in parts of Asia. It also says shellfish is a leading adult/older-child anaphylaxis trigger in Hong Kong, Taiwan, Singapore, and Thailand. These should remain **country-specific extensions**, not be promoted to a uniform Asia-wide rank.

## Recommended use in the Life Goods research sequence

Use PMC9282141 as a secondary source supporting this provisional research vocabulary:

```text
milk
peanut
egg
crustaceans
fish
wheat
buckwheat
tree_nuts
```

Keep `crustaceans` separate from `molluscs`; the source Table E1 specifically labels its pooled category “Shrimp/crustaceans,” even though PMC9282141 shortens it to “shellfish.” Do not infer that the number covers all molluscs.

Track these separately rather than treating them as Asia-wide core groups:

```text
bird_nest  # Singapore/Malaysia report; locally important/unique
ant_egg    # northern Thailand report
chickpea   # India report
royal_jelly # Hong Kong/other localized reports
```

For Cambodia, label this list `regional_proxy` and require additional Cambodian or Southeast Asian evidence before assigning local priority. Neither PMC9282141 nor the prevalence studies behind its Asia paragraph include Cambodia.

## Decision

This website is a legitimate **starting source** for identifying candidate allergen groups. It should be cited as a review/synthesis and paired with the 2021 systematic review and country studies above. It should not be cited as:

- an original dataset;
- evidence of Cambodia-specific prevalence;
- a complete list of Asian allergens;
- proof of one stable ranking across Asian countries or age groups; or
- an ingredient-to-allergen mapping source.
